Save or load a design, then expose a progressive RGB thin-lens photo on the GPU.

Built-in framing fills more of the photo. Current Studio orbit and zoom sets the first frame; an orbit takes evenly spaced views around the vertical axis. Exposure time applies to each frame, so 8 frames at 10 minutes take about 80 minutes plus setup and encoding. Subsecond drafts still take at least one GPU pass per frame, plus setup and AVI encoding. The Phong preview stays interactive. The Spectral thin-lens tracer preserves linear HDR for each RGB frame. The server keeps the finished JPEG AVI animation for 24 hours.

The selected ribbons, confetti, or tubes fit inside the die and displace its body material. Choose a transparent outer material to see them. Bevel, face recess, and raised marks alter the closed die mesh and its moments of inertia. The bevel generates radius-accurate edge fillets and corner caps at sampled vertices; triangle density controls visible smoothness. Keeping mark peaks inside the rim equalizes the support profile; statistical fairness is not established.

Physical properties

Contact frequency controls the soft-sphere spring: wall stiffness is particle mass × frequency², and particle-pair stiffness uses reduced mass. These are relative effective DEM presets, not measured Young moduli. The 1/360 s explicit solver caps frequency at 200; literal glass, metal and ceramic contact stiffness would require a much smaller step or another contact model. Changing contact settings restarts the particle preview.

Locked

Closed triangle volume integrals provide the first and second mass moments, center of mass, and moment of inertia tensor. Separate inclusions displace shell material. Enamel uses a thin-film area estimate. Dice marking material uses Pluck optics; raised and recessed relief currently retains body density, so a differing mark density is unresolved. Dice rolling needs polyhedron contact dynamics.

Preview motion

Visual preview moves the displayed object only; it is the cheap close-up option. Particle simulation runs 1–16 copies of the current design in the same compiled toy course as Marble Madness: ramps, throat, and pegs have native wall contacts. Hold the camera-oriented D-pad to tilt its gravity direction. Marble perspective follows particle 0 while the course moves around it. Every copy shares the selected radius, density, friction, restitution, and rolling settings. Different saved designs need a verified four-species assignment; the current law still uses spherical contact and scalar moment of inertia.

Particle simulation loads when selected. It uses the compiled sphere-contact law; modeled dice appearance does not yet supply shape contact.

Lighting

Emitter spread samples five Pluck point lights and changes the Lambertian transition and specular highlight. Spot hardness changes the shader's inner/outer cone gap. The Lambertian cosine law itself has no hardness parameter. Cast-shadow softness is unavailable in this preview.
